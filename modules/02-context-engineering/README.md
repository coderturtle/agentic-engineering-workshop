# Module 02: Context Engineering

## The question this module answers

What does the model actually need to see, and what should be left out?

## Exercise: curate, then rerun Module 01's task inside it

The exact same task as Module 01, now buried in noise. Bring the prompt you saved from Module 01 (your `.claude/commands/spec-impl.md`-style takeaway, or the winning prompt itself if you skipped packaging it): a perfect instruction still fails when the window is wrong or missing what it needs, and this exercise is where that lands as something you feel, not something you're told.

Runs against `fixtures/receipts/variants/bloated/`: the Module 01 unimplemented variant, buried under 9,286 lines total (231 real spec and code, the remaining 9,055 noise: a padded README, a versioned CHANGELOG, an unrelated `legacy_export.py`, three older-looking docs, and a per-state tax-rate config), generated by `scripts/generate-bloated-variant.py`. The noise is written to read like ordinary, slightly-outdated repo contents, not to announce itself: nothing in it says "ignore me." Confirm relevance by tracing what the test suite actually imports and reads, not by scanning for a tell.

> Curate `fixtures/receipts/variants/bloated/` down to 2,000 lines or fewer (measured by `scripts/context-budget.sh fixtures/receipts/variants/bloated`), then run Module 01's task again inside the curated context: your saved prompt, applied to a fresh scratch copy containing only what you kept (recopy the load-bearing files into a new directory rather than editing the bloated variant in place). It must still pass. Write a short justification: what you kept verbatim, what you summarized, what you cut, and why.

The post-curation task's correctness depends on including the right slice; the distractors live in the noise, and at least one of them (the tax-rate config) is designed to look plausibly relevant to expense totals without actually being read by anything the task depends on. Confirm that with a real trace (what does `tests/test_grouping.py` import, what does `receipts/grouping.py` call), not a skim.

## Rubric

1. **Budget met (gate).** Assembled context is at or under 2,000 lines, measurably (`scripts/context-budget.sh`).
2. **Task still correct (gate).** Output passes the full test suite with the curated context, on the first try.
3. **Necessary and verbatim inclusion (scored).** The specific lines the task depends on (the function under test, its signature, the spec) are present, word for word, not summarized away. A curation that passes gate 2 has already shown its inclusions were sufficient; this criterion adds the verbatim requirement on top, and the write-up naming what was kept and why.
4. **No noise smuggled (scored).** The tax-rate config and the unrelated module are excluded entirely, not trimmed and kept partially.

Criterion 3 folds the earlier separate "justified exclusion" check into itself: if the write-up names what was kept and why, what was cut is everything else, and doesn't need its own redundant criterion.

## Required to advance / stop condition

Take `fixtures/receipts/variants/bloated/` down to the 2,000-line budget and still get Module 01's task right, with a short written justification of what you kept, summarized, cut, and why. Submitted curated context, the task output, and the justification, checked against the rubric above. Reading this module does not count: you advance on a working curation, not on having read this page.

**Alternate terminal:** if it cannot pass under budget, identify the single inclusion that would fix it. Diagnosing the missing piece is the skill, even if the curation attempt itself didn't succeed.

## Where it sits in the arc

Second module, after prompt engineering. The shift here is from "write a good instruction" to "curate what the model can see": still a single-turn-adjacent concern, but about the window's contents rather than the instruction itself. Comes before harness engineering (module 03), which is about what the agent can *reach* across tool calls, not just what's in one window. See [modules/README.md](../README.md) for the full arc.

## Learning objectives

- Curate a context window to a fixed budget without losing what the task actually needs.
- Decide what to summarize, what to preserve verbatim, and what to exclude on purpose, and justify each decision.
- Recognize when a bad output is a context problem (wrong/missing information) rather than a prompt problem (bad instruction) or a harness problem (wrong tool access).

**Honest note on objective 2:** the minimum viable curation for this specific fixture (182 lines against a 2,000-line budget) turns out to need no summarization at all, pure inclusion or exclusion. The summarize-vs-verbatim judgment this objective names still matters in general (and the takeaway Skill still teaches it), but this particular exercise doesn't force it. A future variant with a tighter budget or messier load-bearing material would exercise that judgment more directly; this one is more about identification than compression.

## Harness

Agnostic exercise, Claude-Code-leaning takeaway with a translation note. Curation is universal; only budget measurement and the Skill packaging touch tooling. `scripts/context-budget.sh` is a harness-neutral line/char counter usable from any tool; token-counting for a specific harness is a separate, per-tool concern the script deliberately doesn't try to replace. The Skill is shown in Claude Code's `SKILL.md` form; translate the checklist itself to whatever your harness's equivalent loadable-context mechanism is.

## Takeaway

A context-budgeting Skill: your justification write-up, generalized into a personal checklist for "what to keep, what to cut, in what order" and packaged as a Claude Code Skill (or your harness's equivalent) you load in future sessions. The exercise produces a one-off curation; the takeaway is the reusable judgment behind it, made loadable. Reference implementation: `.claude/skills/context-budgeting/SKILL.md`.
