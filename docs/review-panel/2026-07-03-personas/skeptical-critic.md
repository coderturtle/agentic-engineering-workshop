# Persona: Skeptical Practitioner / Critic — 2026-07-03

1. **"The harness *is* the classroom. This is the workshop's punchy thesis"** (workshop-design.md, teaching method section). This is asserted, not argued or evidenced. There's no data, prior pilot, or citation showing that running exercises inside a harness produces better learning outcomes than a well-structured written guide with worked examples. It's a differentiation claim dressed as a pedagogical finding. Either cite something (even informal — a prior workshop's feedback) or soften to "our working hypothesis is."

2. **"Harness engineering and loop engineering answer different *kinds* of question, and that's why the split matters pedagogically"** — presented as settled, but the doc's own "Open questions" section admits the split came from reviewing three blog posts, and one source (CodeRabbit) already frames it as one continuous "historical arc," not two discontinuous kinds of question. The confident framing ("the actual hinge," "the real distinction... not cosmetic") oversells a synthesis of three marketing blog posts as if it were independently validated pedagogy.

3. **"Loop engineering became its own discipline once harnesses got good enough that 'give it more tools' stopped being the bottleneck"** — a historical/causal claim stated flatly with no evidence (no dates, no examples of when this shift happened, no named harness that hit this ceiling). Reads as a plausible narrative asserted as fact.

4. **"a real demonstration of the practices being taught, not auto-generated from session logs"** (build log). Nothing elsewhere in these three docs shows *how* the build log will actually demonstrate the taught practices (no sample entry, no mapping between log content and the four-module arc). Right now it's a claim about a deliverable that doesn't exist yet.

5. Sourcing is thin but presented as research: "External research that shaped the harness/loop split" turns out to be three vendor blog posts (Forward Future, LangChain, CodeRabbit) — fine as inspiration, but calling it "Sources reviewed" alongside confident causal claims (#3 above) lends it more authority than three marketing posts warrant. Consider flagging these explicitly as industry blogs, not research, given the "prove it or cut it" bar.

No findings on README.md or workshop-gremlin-design.md — those are largely status/scope statements, not claims of efficacy, and don't oversell.
